Technical scope, checks and references

This is a new resource; the missing earlier file has not been audited. The selected twelve joints cover common school joinery, plus a pinned through-tenon example for discussing Japanese timber joinery. “Finger joint” means a square-finger box corner here, not an industrial end-grain finger splice. Kigumi describes a tradition of timber assembly, not one universal joint.

Dimensions are arbitrary model units. Glue films, machining clearance (except loose inserts), wood movement, grain anisotropy, load capacity and clamping are not simulated. Abutting mesh sections of the same colour form one continuous part; their construction edges are not saw cuts. Reveal mode makes timber translucent; it is not a physical cutaway. The painter-based renderer may have ordering artefacts at intersecting projected faces; use solid mode and alternate views to inspect fit.

These checks support the stated geometry only. They are not structural certification or a substitute for teacher review. Do not use an animation as machine-operation instruction. Set up tools and workholding under your teacher’s supervision.

References: Swedish Wood — Joinery Handbook, chapter 4 (joint families, timber and furniture construction); Lamello — Classic X (4 mm biscuit slots); Lamello — original wood biscuit system (inserts and joining methods); Japan House — KIGUMI (Japanese carpentry context). The wooden-pin model is a generic pinned joint, not a reconstruction of a named historical Japanese joint.
